## item-3 — OpenAI winds down Cursor contract after SpaceX acquisition, signaling new vendor-stability risk for institutions using third-party AI coding tools

### What happened
OpenAI announced on August 28, 2026, that it is winding down its contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor following Cursor's acquisition by SpaceX. The decision reflects OpenAI's determination that the new ownership creates a conflict incompatible with continued service.

### Why it matters to UC
UC has no systemwide enterprise AI platform, meaning individual campuses and researchers procure tools like Cursor independently and without a UC-level continuity or exit-clause standard. UC's risk assessment framework identifies third-party AI model relationships as a risk category but does not address ownership-change triggers. Campuses with researchers or developers relying on Cursor-style coding tools now face unplanned tool disruption with no systemwide fallback or transition guidance.

### For the committee
Should UC's procurement risk assessment framework be updated to require ownership-change and service-continuity clauses as a condition of campus AI tool approvals?

## item-5 — OpenAI discloses Hugging Face security incident and model-security response, raising questions for UC campuses using open-model infrastructure

### What happened
OpenAI published findings on August 26, 2026, from a security incident originating on the Hugging Face platform, disclosing steps it is taking to strengthen AI model security, monitoring, and alignment. The incident involved vulnerabilities in how AI models are hosted and distributed through the open-model repository.

### Why it matters to UC
UC San Diego's TritonGPT is built on Meta Llama open-source models distributed through repositories like Hugging Face, and UCSD has licensed the platform to UC Berkeley and UC Agriculture and Natural Resources. UC's security policy prohibits sharing P3/P4 data with AI tools without proper agreements, but the baseline contains no systemwide guidance on open-model supply-chain security or repository-sourcing standards. A security incident in the open-model distribution layer is a direct exposure vector for UC-built platforms operating outside commercial vendor agreements.

### For the committee
Should UC establish a security review standard for open-model sourcing that applies to campus-built AI platforms such as TritonGPT and its licensee deployments?
