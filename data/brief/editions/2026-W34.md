## item-1 — ED proposes accreditation overhaul under Executive Order 14279, with implications for how institutions demonstrate AI governance quality

### What happened
The Department of Education published a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on August 20, 2026 to revise 34 CFR Part 602, implementing Executive Order 14279 on reforming accreditation. The proposal would restructure how accrediting agencies are recognized, reduce regulatory burden, and realign recognition criteria more closely with statute. Comments will be solicited through the Federal Register process.

### Why it matters to UC
Accreditation standards are one of the primary external levers shaping what institutional AI governance UC campuses must demonstrate. UC has a systemwide responsible AI principles framework and a risk assessment guide, but no systemwide enterprise AI platform and no common accreditation-facing articulation of how campus-level AI governance satisfies quality assurance requirements. If accreditors revise standards in response to the new rules — or if ED narrows what counts as valid accreditor oversight — UC's decentralized, campus-by-campus AI governance posture may need to be documented and harmonized at a level it has not yet been.

### For the committee
Should UCOP proactively map its existing AI governance infrastructure — principles, risk assessment guide, campus councils — against likely revised accreditation quality standards, and file a coordinated UC comment during the rulemaking period?

## item-4 — OpenAI announces Zero Data Retention for frontier models and Private Safety Processing, directly affecting how UC campuses can characterize data protection in their existing OpenAI agreements

### What happened
On August 19, 2026, OpenAI announced Zero Data Retention (ZDR) for eligible API customers on frontier models and previewed Private Safety Processing — a mechanism that allows advanced safety monitoring without retaining customer data. The announcement reaffirms that ZDR is available for API use and extends it to newer model tiers.

### Why it matters to UC
Multiple UC campuses have active OpenAI agreements: UCLA deployed ChatGPT Enterprise in September 2024, UCSF is transitioning to ChatGPT Enterprise replacing Versa Chat with P3/P4 data approval, and UC Davis is implementing ChatGPT Edu. Each campus's data-handling representations to its community — and the adequacy of those representations against UC IS-3 and P3/P4 classification requirements — depend on the contractual terms in force. UC has no systemwide enterprise AI platform and no single point of accountability for tracking how vendor policy changes propagate into campus-level data agreements. The OGC AI Alert prohibits sharing personal information with AI tools without a fully executed agreement covering UC data; the new ZDR and Private Safety Processing features may alter what agreements need to say, but no systemwide mechanism exists to push that update to campuses.

### For the committee
Does UCOP have a process to assess and communicate to campuses when a major vendor's data-retention policy change requires review or amendment of existing agreements under IS-3 and the OGC AI Alert?

## item-5 — Chancellor Khosla's public interview articulates UC San Diego's rationale for building its own AI — a posture the committee has not yet adopted or evaluated systemwide

### What happened
On August 19, 2026, the California Learning Lab published a transcript of 'My Robot Teacher' Episode 17, an interview with UC San Diego Chancellor and UCOP AI Steering Committee co-chair Pradeep Khosla. Khosla explains UCSD's decision to build TritonGPT rather than rely on commercial vendors, citing data sovereignty, cost (approximately 40% cheaper over three years), and institutional control as primary drivers, and discusses his view of when public universities should build and govern AI infrastructure for themselves.

### Why it matters to UC
TritonGPT is the only UC-built, UC-operated AI platform in the system; every other campus relies on vendor contracts. The committee's co-chair has now articulated publicly a build-vs-buy doctrine grounded in data sovereignty and cost — but the committee has no systemwide position on that doctrine, no comparative cost analysis across campus models, and no policy on when campuses should seek vendor agreements versus building locally. UCSD has licensed TritonGPT to UC Berkeley (BearGPT) and UC Agriculture and Natural Resources, suggesting a potential UC-internal sharing model that is not governed by any systemwide framework. The AI Council Risk Assessment Guide explicitly notes the absence of a common UC generative AI platform as a gap UC Davis has also flagged, estimating continued local development at approximately $25,000 per year in token costs plus one FTE.

### For the committee
Should the committee formally evaluate a UC-internal platform-sharing model anchored on TritonGPT — including data sovereignty, cost, and equity-of-access implications — as an alternative to parallel vendor negotiations at each campus?
